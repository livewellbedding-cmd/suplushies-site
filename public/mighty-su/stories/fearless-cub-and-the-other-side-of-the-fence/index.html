Home and family

Fearless Cub and the Other Side of the Fence

3 min read · Series 1: The Mighty Zoo · Starring Fearless Cub

Every evening, Fearless Cub pressed his nose to the tall green fence at the edge of the Mighty Zoo. On the other side, the city hummed. Car horns honked like geese. Food carts smelled like popcorn and adventure. The lights blinked on, one by one, as if they were calling his name.

“I bet it’s amazing out there,” said Chaos Cat, stretching on the fence post beside him. “No bedtime. No rules. Grass as far as you can run.”

Fearless Cub’s tail swished. He was the one who stood in front of everyone when things got scary. Surely he was brave enough for the big world.

The next morning, a delivery truck rolled through the service gate. The gate swung open. And before he could talk himself out of it, Fearless Cub slipped through.

At first, it was everything Chaos had promised. He chased pigeons in the park. He watched a street musician play a shiny trumpet. He rolled in grass that looked so green from the zoo.

Up close, the grass was dry and scratchy. Nobody knew his name. Nobody saved him a seat. And when the sky turned the color of an old bruise, nobody called him in for dinner.

Then came the storm. Thunder cracked. Rain poured sideways. Fearless Cub squeezed under a park bench, soaked to his whiskers, and for the first time all day, he didn’t feel fearless at all.

“Cub! CUB!”

Through the rain came a familiar shape: Workhorse, his tool belt clanking, a tarp held high over his head like a roof. Right behind him, dripping and shivering, was Chaos Cat.

“I told you it was amazing out here,” Chaos said in a small voice. “I’d never actually been. I’m sorry.”

Fearless Cub didn’t say “I told you so,” and Workhorse didn’t say “You should have known better.” He just lifted the tarp and made room for two more under it.

Back home, Generous Piggy had a dry blanket waiting, and the hay smelled like every good night he’d ever had. Fearless Cub curled up between his family and listened to the rain drum on the roof. It sounded much friendlier from in here.

By the gate, the Zookeeper latched the lock twice, glanced back at the little pile of sleepy animals and smiled. They didn’t say a word. They didn’t need to.

The lesson

Be curious, and go explore. But the grass isn’t always greener on the other side. Home isn’t only where your heart is; it’s your shelter from the storm. When the sky turns dark, bet on family.
